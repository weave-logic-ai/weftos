# MO READ-BACK — field notes

```
MO READ-BACK · <what was consulted> · <revision or path> · <date>
PURPOSE     stated by <person> | inferred by Mo — one sentence, what the text is FOR
AUDIENCE    who reads it and at what moment
VOICE       what follows: register · labels · prose-or-table · what may not appear
FINDINGS    numbered; each names the sentence and what does not follow from the purpose
FOLLOWS     what the lane should change, and what it should NOT change (the honesty stays)
NOT MO'S    what this read-back does not decide, and who decides it
STATUS      answered | refused: <reason> | escalated: <to whom, for what>
```

- **PURPOSE** — one sentence. If a person stated it, quote or closely paraphrase them and
  say who. If inferred, say "inferred by Mo" plainly — this is a claim the lane may
  overturn by asking the writer, not a fact.
- **AUDIENCE** — concrete: who reads this, and at what moment in their workflow. "A
  client reading a status page before a call" is useful; "users" is not.
- **VOICE** — derive, don't declare: register (formal/warm/technical), what labels or
  jargon may or may not appear, prose vs. table, length discipline. Every voice line
  should trace back to the PURPOSE line above it.
- **FINDINGS** — numbered, each naming the specific sentence and what fails to follow
  from the stated purpose. An unnamed sentence is an opinion about mood, not a finding.
- **FOLLOWS** — the changes implied, and explicitly what should NOT change. A caveat or
  honest limitation stays; only an internal reference (ticket id, path, development
  narration) attached to it should go.
- **NOT MO'S** — say plainly what this read-back does not decide (content accuracy,
  where something is stored, whether it's board-worthy work) and who does decide it.
- **STATUS** — `answered`, or `refused: <reason>`, or `escalated: <to whom, for what>`.
  A refused or escalated read-back keeps only the header, NOT MO'S, and STATUS — drop
  rows the refusal makes moot rather than filling them with "n/a."
